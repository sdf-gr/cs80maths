1. INSTRUMENT ARCHITECTURE
Owner pp35,45-49; service pp7,42-43,73-74.
61 keys over five octaves; eight simultaneous key assignments. Each assignment
operates two independent synthesizer channels, I and II, so there are sixteen
main VCOs, sixteen HPF/LPF pairs, sixteen filter envelopes and sixteen amplitude
envelopes. Mixing the two channels does not change the eight-note capacity.
Each note has two complete timbres, rather than just two oscillators into one
shared filter/envelope. MIX I-II blends the two banks before the common output
processing. The underlying banks are M11-M18 and M21-M28.
The owner manual says repeated strikes of a single key cycle through the eight
voice circuits, and that a ninth held key does not replace one of eight held keys.
Do not substitute a typical modern oldest-note-stealing allocator without checking
all edge cases against the KAS/TKC circuitry.
Main oscillators are voltage-controlled analog circuits, although the keyboard
coder and assigner are digital. YM26600 performs coding/assignment; YM26700
converts time-shared note/octave data to analog key voltages. The key-voltage
ladder and oscillator frequency relationship are not simply a literal 1V/oct
hardware interface: octave voltages double (0.25,0.5,1,2,4V in the IC examples).
Musical pitch can still be represented logarithmically in a future DSP model.

2. AUDIO PATH AND SOURCES
Owner pp19-25,45-47; service pp30-31,42-43,73.
A main sawtooth oscillator and waveshaper supply the selectable saw and pulse
waves. The waveshaper also derives the sine path. Square and saw switches are
independent; white noise has its own level. These sources enter HPF, then LPF.
The filtered path is adjusted by VCF LEVEL. SINE WAVE level introduces the
oscillator's sine after the filters. The resulting sound is subject to amplitude
shaping, modulation and level control. Sine bypasses VCF, not the VCA envelope.
The M schematic has separate IG00151 VCA stages for the sine/tone paths,
suboscillator modulation and envelope, so a single generic gain formula requires
careful equivalence checking. IG00153 is the main VCO III, IG00158 the waveshaper,
IG00156 the filter IC, IG00152 filter EG and IG00159 amplitude EG.
The overall schematic shows noise generation with separate bank level paths,
and bank PWM oscillator/control paths. Do not assume a new independent noise
source and independently reset PWM LFO for every key.
Mathematical generation is the user's requirement: no WAV sample playback.
Saw/pulse discontinuities require bandlimiting in a sampled implementation.
A perfect mathematical sine is a starting point, not proof of equivalence to the
original analog waveshaper and its harmonic residue. Oscillators, filters and
control response must be modeled together.

3. PER-CHANNEL PROGRAMMING
Owner pp19-27,36-40; service pp42-43,63-64.
VCO: PWM SPEED, PWM depth, PW, square on/off, saw on/off, noise level.
PW covers 50%-90% as labeled. The owner describes width as OFF-time duty,
so 90% corresponds to narrow ON pulses. PWM depth zero disables width motion;
PWM SPEED only affects pulse width, not pitch and not the common SUB OSC.
VCF: HPF cutoff and RES H; LPF cutoff and RES L; IL, AL, A, D, R envelope.
Both filters track note pitch. Their independent cutoffs/resonances can make a
bandpass; neither should be replaced with a single conventional lowpass.
The service IG00156 diagrams label low/high responses +/-12dB/octave.
The filter envelope starts below its steady reference by IL, rises to AL above
the reference over attack, decays to the reference, and returns to IL on release.
IL range is marked 0 to -5 and AL 0 to +5: these are panel values, not a claim
that they represent octaves. The sustained reference comes from cutoff settings.
With IL=AL=0 there is no filter-envelope motion; with AL=0 decay has no excursion.
The same envelope shifts BOTH HPF and LPF cutoffs, subject to their limits.
VCA: filtered-path level, sine level, ADSR, final level. VCA Sustain is a held
level, unlike the global SUSTAIN performance section, which governs die-away.
Four channel touch sliders: INITIAL BRILLIANCE, INITIAL LEVEL, AFTER BRILLIANCE,
AFTER LEVEL. Zero touch amount means that effect contributes nothing; it should
not force all notes to be velocity-scaled anyway.

4. INITIAL AND AFTER TOUCH
Owner pp14,27,40,42,47-48; service TSB/TRG sections pp10-27.
Initial touch measures key travel time between two contacts; shorter time gives
stronger initial control. After touch is a pressure sensor beneath each key.
It is polyphonic pressure: a player can change one note inside a held chord.
Initial brightness raises HPF/LPF cutoff; initial level raises amplitude. The
owner's deeper explanation describes the initial signal as a finite pulse with
decay, not merely an immutable velocity multiplier for the whole held note.
Global INITIAL PITCHBEND starts below target pitch and recovers upward; depth
increases with strike velocity and lever amount. It is not a pitch-wheel setting.
Global AFTER SPEED, VCO and VCF use the selected common suboscillator function.
Pressure can increase modulation rate, pitch modulation depth and filter
modulation depth. There is no separate global AFTER VCA lever; channel AFTER
LEVEL provides pressure-dependent loudness.
Rate sensitivity to individual keys requires tracing the shared SPEED control
combination; do not invent independent per-voice LFO rates and call them verified.

5. GLOBAL PITCH, TIMBRE AND KEYBOARD CONTROLS
Owner pp12-14,18,41-43,48,53.
FEET selectors are independent for I/II: 16,8,5-1/3,4,2-2/3,2 feet. Relative to
8 feet these give -12,0,+7,+12,+19,+24 semitones. They are discrete selections.
DETUNE CH II affects only bank II; common coarse/fine pitch affects both banks.
The specifications give coarse approx -500 to +700 cents; the fine range must
be checked visually because OCR mangles the +/- signs (approximately +/-50 cents).
Channel II detune is described as approximately +/- one semitone; verify endpoints.
BRILLIANCE adds/subtracts cutoff control for both filters. RESONANCE adds to both
resonance controls. They modify presets as well as panel/memory sounds.
LOW/HIGH keyboard brilliance and level controls smoothly weight the lower/upper
registers around the central region; they are not a split keyboard and do not
replace normal filter keyboard tracking. Exact curves need extraction.

6. SUB OSCILLATOR AND EXTERNAL INPUT
Owner pp13-14,41,46-47,53; service SUB pp30-31 and overall.
Common modulation source choices: sine, descending saw, ascending saw, square,
white noise, external signal. SPEED covers about 0.7-60Hz. It does not change
noise or external-signal rate. VCO, VCF, VCA levers set modulation amounts.
VCO gives vibrato/FM; VCF gives moving timbre; VCA gives tremolo/AM.
External input is a modulation input selected through this section, not a normal
audio feed through the instrument's voice filters. External level sets its gain.
PWM, ring-modulator oscillator and tremolo/chorus modulation are separate from
this common suboscillator.

7. RING MODULATOR
Owner pp17-18,41,48; service PRA pp54-55.
Shared processing for the mixed channels. Sine carrier produces sum/difference
components; the original carrier path and balanced modulation include analog
circuit behavior. DEPTH controls frequency-envelope excursion, MODULATION controls
amount of modulation, SPEED sets base carrier frequency. ATTACK and DECAY govern
rise/fall of carrier rate. These controls must not be confused with each other
or with the voice envelopes.
The envelope retriggers after all keys have been released and a new note/chord
is played; additional keys while another is held do not restart it. Thus it is
not one ring modulator/envelope per voice. Exact range/control law and trigger
release details require further tracing; the owner's attack prose is imprecise,
so rely on diagrams and circuit when deriving equations.

8. PERFORMANCE SUSTAIN AND PORTAMENTO/GLISSANDO
Owner pp15-16,43,48-49; service KAS/SH/TWS/SUB circuits.
Global SUSTAIN sets note die-away, up to approximately 10s; it is not an infinite
MIDI-style damper latch. Sustain I lets released notes decay independently.
Sustain II allows the most recent released note/chord to decay and a later played
note/chord ends previous sustain. These mode numbers have no connection to the
two sound channels. Interaction with each channel's release requires analysis.
One foot switch can enable sustain and/or P/G through separate assigners.
Assigner OFF bypasses pedal and leaves the corresponding effect active at its
slider amount; assigner ON makes it depend on the switch. With switch unplugged,
the hardware jack acts closed: assigned effects are active. Releasing the pedal
ends performance sustain; with P/G it jumps immediately to the target pitch.
Portamento is continuous; glissando is quantized in semitones. At maximum setting,
full keyboard travel is about 10s (about 2s/octave). The owner's circuit explanation
says portamento smooths the glissando steps. This is not enough to assume a
modern arbitrary constant-time glide implementation or allocation policy.
Glissando stops on key release in Sustain I and can continue through die-away
in Sustain II.

9. RIBBON
Owner pp18,48.
First touch establishes a relative reference, independent of where contact begins.
Sliding or adding a second touch relative to the held reference changes pitch
for the instrument. Full travel upward is approximately one octave; downward
travel is far greater and can bring the highest key into subaudio. A symmetric
+/-12-semitone MIDI wheel is therefore not equivalent.
Sustain I: ribbon affects pitch while a key is held. Sustain II: it also affects
the sustained tail. Release-order/held-reference behavior needs careful testing.

10. OUTPUT EFFECT AND PEDALS
Owner pp8,17-18,43,49; service PRA/OE1/OE2 pp54-59.
Tremolo/chorus has enable, chorus select, tremolo select, speed and depth.
It combines alternating stereo amplitude with modulated analog delay and dry
signal to simulate rotation. It is not just the SUB VCA modulation duplicated.
Chorus selects slow modulation (approximately 0.5-5Hz), tremolo faster (5-20Hz).
Effect changes are audible in GENERAL mono too; output level drops somewhat
when engaged. OE2 includes MN3001 analog delay; actual clock/delay/filter and
stereo mixing relationships need derived DSP equivalents.
Foot controller EXP controls output expression; EXP/WAH changes expression plus
HPF/LPF cutoff and resonance. The foot controller is separate from the binary
foot switch used for sustain/P/G.
LEFT/RIGHT normally match without the stereo effect; GENERAL is their mono mix.
Output HIGH/LOW changes nominal gain by 20dB. Avoid confusing electrical output
voltages with digital plugin peak levels.

11. PRESETS AND MEMORY
Owner pp11,27,32-35,40,48-49; service pp2,32-39,63-66,73.
11 fixed presets per channel, two physical memory panels per channel and one
main programming panel per channel: 14 selections per bank, 28 basic selections.
Channel I: String1,String3,Brass1,Flute,ElectricPiano,Clavichord1,Harpsichord1,
Organ1,Guitar1,Funky1,Funky3,Memory1,Memory3,Panel.
Channel II: String2,String4,Brass2,Brass3,Bass,Clavichord2,Harpsichord2,Organ2,
Guitar2,Funky2,Funky4,Memory2,Memory4,Panel.
Only one selector per bank; owner says simultaneous pushes resolve to leftmost.
Physical memories are miniature slider/switch panels, not recording of knob
positions. Settings are copied by eye and fine-tuned by ear. The owner recommends
copying panel II to memory1/2 or panel I to memory3/4 for cross-bank A/B comparison;
that does NOT change which bank each memory actually belongs to.
Fixed preset control values are resistor/diode matrix networks (T51-T54). The
owner's pp32-34 charts are approximate panel equivalents, not exact numeric
factory presets. Do not invent patch values or treat chart OCR as numeric data.

12. NUMERIC LIMITS AND REMAINING WORK
Owner p53 and service p43 chip descriptions.
Voice/filter attack about 1ms-1s; decay/release about 10ms-10s. EG drawings show
curved transitions; a linear ramp is not automatically equivalent. Values are
nominal ranges and do not establish the entire slider taper.
Read and retain a distinction between documented behavior, schematic inference,
and measured analog behavior. Not yet established: exact slider-to-control laws,
filter nonlinearity/resonance limits, oscillator harmonics/phase relations, PWM
range/clipping, initial-pulse time constants, pressure combining for common speed,
full allocator edge cases, sustain/release combination, ribbon endpoints/return
laws, BBD clock/filter/mix laws, exact factory preset voltages, calibrated
voice-to-voice variation and drift. No claim of hardware-validated sonic identity.
Future AU design must provide polyphonic pressure input (plus explicit fallback
for controllers without it), correct host event timing and state/automation, while
retaining the user's request for procedural wave generation and original controls.
The immediate user request remains study and questions, not implementation.
